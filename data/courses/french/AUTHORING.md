# Authoring brief for the French course

Read `docs/course-format.md` first: it is the complete, authoritative format.
Then read one Spanish lesson as the exemplar of tone, depth and shape:
`data/courses/spanish/lessons/a1/ser-vs-estar.json`. The French course has
the same quality bar and the same features.

## Quality bar for every lesson

- **Accuracy first.** Standard metropolitan French, correct accents and
  cedillas (é/è/ê, à/a, où/ou, ça), correct elision (`j'ai`, `l'école`,
  `qu'il`), correct gender and agreement, correct contractions (`au`, `aux`,
  `du`, `des`). **Straight apostrophes only (`'`), never `’`**, in every
  field. Explanations in plain English, with the French in **bold**.
  French punctuation: a space before `?`, `!`, `:` and `;` in French
  sentences (`Ça va ?`); none in English text.
- `content`: 3–5 `## ` sections, each one idea, short paragraphs, tables for
  paradigms, bullet lists for examples with translations. No section longer
  than ~120 words. A closing section may be titled "Quick check" only if it
  has no exercises (Sprint mode skips sections with that heading).
- `vocabulary`: 10–14 entries with `term`, `meaning`, an `example` sentence
  and its `example_meaning`. Nouns carry their article (`la gare`,
  `l'école (f)` is NOT the style: write `l'école` and put the gender in the
  meaning: `school (f)`). Verbs as infinitives. Terms unique inside the
  lesson.
- `quiz`: 9–11 questions, using the types like this: 3–4 `multiple-choice`
  (2–4 options, exactly one correct, the others plausible but definitely
  wrong), 3–4 `fill-in-blank` (write `___` where the answer goes; list every
  reasonable `accepted_answers` variant, e.g. with/without the subject
  pronoun, `j'ai`/`ai`), 1–2 `true-false` (a statement about the grammar or
  a sentence's correctness; `correct_answer` is `"true"` or `"false"`), and
  `numeric` where the syllabus table says so (a number the French sentence
  states: a price, a time, a count; `correct_answer` in digits; `unit` and
  `tolerance` where they fit; the prompt may be in English or French). No
  `expression` questions: they are for formulas. Every question has an
  `explanation` and at least one hint. `question_id` = `<lesson id>-01`,
  `-02`, … `teaches` lists only terms of this lesson's vocabulary.
- `conjugations` only where the table assigns them: `item`, `meaning`,
  `set` (`present`, `passe-compose`, `imparfait`, `futur`, `conditionnel`,
  `subjonctif`), all six `forms` (`je`, `tu`, `il`, `nous`, `vous`, `ils`).
  Forms are written **without the subject pronoun** but **with** any
  reflexive pronoun: for `s'appeler` the forms are `m'appelle`,
  `t'appelles`, `s'appelle`, `nous appelons`, `vous appelez`, `s'appellent`.
  Compound tenses are two words: `ai parlé`, `suis allé`, `sommes allés`
  (masculine agreement is the drill's canonical form; say so in the
  content). Give every entry a 4–6 line
  ASCII `art` (≤ 24 chars per line, plain ASCII only). An item + set pair may
  be taught by ONE lesson in the whole course; the tables reserve them.
- `conversations`: one dialogue of 8–12 lines, two named characters, each
  line with `text` and `meaning`, a `scenario`, an `id` like
  `<lesson id>-conv-<slug>`.
- `listening`: 8 sentences, short to long (4–14 words), each with `text`
  (accents in place, numbers written as words, no digits, quotes, slashes
  or parentheses) and `meaning`. Every sentence practises the lesson's point
  with its own vocabulary; two or three slip in one common new word and say
  so in a `note` of the exact shape `New word: la gare = the station.`; the
  others may carry a one-line grammar pointer as the note, or none. Avoid
  sentences the ear cannot settle (homophones: `vers/vert/verre`, `a/à`
  out of context, `ces/ses/c'est/s'est`). Texts distinct within the lesson,
  and never a copy of a quiz prompt, build or vocabulary example
  (`listening_does_not_copy_builds_or_examples` checks it).
  Do not copy quiz prompts, builds or vocabulary examples.
- `guide`: hand-ordered, 12–17 steps: `{"section": "<heading prefix>"}`,
  `{"vocab": n}` (hands of 3–5 that together deal the whole vocabulary),
  `{"question": "<id>"}` interleaved (every question of the lesson once),
  one or two `{"tip": "…"}`, and exactly one `{"concept": {"title": "…",
  "markdown": "…"}}` card (an extra idea that lives only in the Guide, ≤ 60
  words). Every section appears once.
- `sprint`: `icon` (the emoji from the table), 3–4 `bites` (title, emoji,
  rule ≤ 200 chars, example + example_meaning), 2–3 `sorts` (2–4 buckets,
  4–8 items, `why`), 4–6 `spots` (a sentence with exactly one wrong word;
  `wrong` must appear exactly once in the sentence, punctuation aside;
  `fix`; `why`), 3–5 `builds` (text of 3–9 words, meaning, 1–2
  distractors).
- Pretty-printed JSON, 2-space indent, UTF-8, trailing newline.

### Validate after every file

```sh
cargo test --quiet -- content::tests sprint::tests 2>&1 | tail -5
```

A validation error names the file and field; fix it before moving on. The
Rust tests load every course, so a broken file of another author shows up
too: fix only your own files and report the rest.

## Icons

Every lesson has an icon, its `sprint.icon`, unique across the whole course,
and none is the locked icon (🔒). Use exactly the icons in the tables; do not
improvise.

## The syllabus

Files go in `data/courses/french/lessons/<unit>/<slug>.json`, `unit` =
`"A1"` … `"B2"`, ids `<unit>-<slug>`. Group ids are `Grammar` and
`Vocabulary` (exact spelling). The `conjugations` column reserves item/set
pairs: teach exactly those, nowhere else. **numeric n** = the quiz has n
`numeric` questions.

The course has 75 lessons, sized like the Spanish course's A1–B2 (#582):
21 in A1, 19 in A2, 18 in B1 and 17 in B2. The first 34 (#464) sit at
orders 10, 14, 18… in each unit; the lessons added for #582 take the even
orders between them, so a new lesson slots in without renumbering. A new
lesson's prerequisites only name lessons earlier on the path.
