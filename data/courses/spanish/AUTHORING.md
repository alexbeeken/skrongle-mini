# Authoring brief for the Spanish course

Read `docs/course-format.md` first: it is the complete, authoritative format.
Then read two existing lessons as exemplars of tone and depth:
`data/courses/spanish/lessons/a1/ser-vs-estar.json` and
`data/courses/spanish/lessons/a2/regular-preterite-ar.json`.

## Quality bar for every lesson

- **Accuracy first.** Standard Castilian Spanish (vosotros included), correct
  accents (está/esta, él/el, sí/si, qué/que in questions…), correct gender
  agreement. Explanations in plain English, with the Spanish in **bold**.
- `content`: 3–5 `## ` sections, each one idea, short paragraphs, use tables
  for paradigms and bullet lists for examples with translations. No section
  longer than ~120 words. A closing section may be titled "Quick check" only if
  it has no exercises (Sprint mode skips sections with that heading).
- `vocabulary`: 10–14 entries with term, meaning, an `example` sentence and
  its `example_meaning` (the English, shown when the learner hovers the
  example). Terms must be unique inside the lesson. Verbs as infinitives.
- `quiz`: 8–10 questions, roughly half `multiple-choice` (2–4 options,
  exactly one correct, the others plausible but definitely wrong) and half
  `fill-in-blank` (write `___` where the answer goes; list every reasonable
  `accepted_answers` variant such as with/without the subject pronoun). Every
  question has an `explanation` and at least one hint. `question_id` =
  `<lesson id>-01`, `-02`, … `teaches` lists only terms of this lesson's
  vocabulary.
- `conjugations` only where the brief assigns them: `item`, `meaning`,
  `set` (`present`, `preterite`, `imperfect`, `future`, `conditional`,
  `present-subjunctive`), all six `forms` (`yo`, `tu`, `el`, `nosotros`,
  `vosotros`, `ellos`). Give each item a 4–6 line ASCII `art` (≤ 24 chars per
  line, plain ASCII only). An item + set pair may be taught by ONE lesson in
  the whole course; the brief reserves them.
- `conversations`: one dialogue of 8–12 lines, two named characters, each
  line with `text` and `meaning`, a `scenario`, an `id` like
  `<lesson id>-conv-<slug>`.
- `guide`: hand-ordered, 12–17 steps: `{"section": "<heading prefix>"}`,
  `{"vocab": n}` (hands of 3–5), `{"question": "<id>"}` interleaved, one or
  two `{"tip": "…"}`. Every section and question should appear once.
- `sprint`: `icon` (one emoji), 3–4 `bites` (title, emoji, rule ≤ 200 chars,
  example + example_meaning), 2–3 `sorts` (2–4 buckets, 4–8 items, `why`),
  4–6 `spots` (a sentence with exactly one wrong word; `wrong` must appear
  exactly once in the sentence, punctuation aside; `fix`; `why`), 3–5
  `builds` (text of 3–9 words, meaning, 1–2 distractors).
- `listening`: 8 sentences, short to long (4–14 words), each with `text`
  (standard Castilian, accents and ¿¡ in place, numbers written as words, no
  digits, quotes, slashes or parentheses) and `meaning`. Every sentence
  practises the lesson's point with its own vocabulary; two or three slip in
  one common new word and say so in a `note` of the exact shape
  `New word: la oficina = the office.`; the others may carry a one-line
  grammar pointer as the note, or none. Avoid sentences whose spelling the
  ear cannot settle (hay/ahí, a ver/haber, tuvo/tubo). Do not copy quiz
  prompts, builds or vocabulary examples (the content test
  `listening_does_not_copy_builds_or_examples` checks it).
- Pretty-printed JSON, 2-space indent, UTF-8, trailing newline.

Validate after every file:

```sh
cargo test --quiet -- content::tests::loads_the_builtin_spanish_course sprint::tests::every_lesson_builds_a_session 2>&1 | tail -5
```

A validation error names the file and field; fix it before moving on.

## The B1 syllabus (CEFR B1 / Plan Curricular del Instituto Cervantes)

Files go in `data/courses/spanish/lessons/b1/<slug>.json`, `unit` = `"B1"`,
ids `b1-<slug>`. B1 is where the PCIC puts the **present subjunctive** (all
six forms and its everyday triggers: wishes, feelings, value judgements,
doubt, future time clauses, purpose, unknown antecedents) and the **simple
conditional** (advice, politeness, hypotheses, the future seen from the
past). The imperfect subjunctive (si tuviera…, quería que vinieras) and the
compound tenses (había hablado, habría hablado, haya hablado) belong to B2:
a B1 lesson may name them in one line as "coming at B2", never teach or
drill them. Conditional sentences stop at real conditions (si + present).

Reserved conjugations already taught elsewhere: the present subjunctive of
hablar, comer, escribir, hacer, poner, decir, ir, venir, tener, salir, ser
(`a2-imperative`); the conditional of gustar, deber, poder, querer
(`a2-obligation-and-advice`). B1 reserves the present subjunctive and
conditional items listed below.

## The B2 syllabus (CEFR B2 / Plan Curricular del Instituto Cervantes)

Files go in `data/courses/spanish/lessons/b2/<slug>.json`, `unit` = `"B2"`,
ids `b2-<slug>`. B2 is where the learner argues, reports and imagines: the
imperfect subjunctive and the sequence of tenses, the compound tenses,
conditional sentences, reported speech, the passive and `se`, relative
clauses with the subjunctive, and the discourse markers and register of
written Spanish. The present subjunctive and the simple conditional belong
to B1; B2 assumes them and recaps only what it builds on. Prerequisites
point at the B1 (or earlier B2) lesson each one builds on; an A2 one stays
only where nothing at B1 is the base (the pluperfect).

Conjugations: the paradigm has no set for the imperfect subjunctive, the
pluperfect subjunctive or any compound tense, so those are taught in
content tables. B2 reserves only the `haber` auxiliary in two sets:
`haber` / `imperfect` (había…) and `haber` / `future` (habré…).
`haber` / `conditional` (habría…) belongs to B1's conditional lesson and
`haber` / `present-subjunctive` (haya…) to B1's irregular subjunctives;
the conditional perfect and perfect subjunctive lessons show **habría**
and **haya** in tables.

## The C1 syllabus (CEFR C1 / Plan Curricular del Instituto Cervantes)

Files go in `data/courses/spanish/lessons/c1/<slug>.json`, `unit` = `"C1"`,
ids `c1-<slug>`. C1 is about nuance: the learner already knows every tense,
so lessons teach the choice between two correct-looking forms, the register
a sentence belongs to, and how a text is built. Explanations stay in plain
English; examples may be longer (up to ~20 words) and idiomatic. The
paradigm has no columns for the compound or imperfect-subjunctive tenses, so
those are taught in content tables (hubiera / hubiese + participle, habría +
participle, voseo forms), never as `conjugations`. Reserved conjugations:
llevar / present, seguir / present (haber / future belongs to the B2 future perfect lesson; C1 shows those forms in a table).

Prerequisites point at the B1/B2 lessons (and earlier C1 ones) each lesson
builds on, never at a later level or a later lesson.

## The C2 syllabus (PCIC C2 / CEFR C2: mastery)

ids `c2-<slug>`, `order` 10–32. C2 is recognition and precision: the
learner already speaks well and now reads literary and legal prose, writes

never quote a published work, a known poem or a famous line.
